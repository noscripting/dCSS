# Changelog

All notable changes to dcss are written here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html): a class, an input, an output, a function or a file removed or changed in meaning is a major version.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `.dcss-summarize`: one list, written once, gives its sum, count, length, mean, min, max, median, variance and deviation (`--dcss-sum` to `--dcss-deviation`). The element after the list is the page's own (a `<p>`, a `<dl>`), and its children get the sum, count, length, mean and min; one element of class `dcss-read` around it adds the max, and two the median, variance and deviation. Up to 64 items in 64ths from -1024 to 65535 (±1024 for the variance); each answer is to the bit in Chrome and Safari (1,948 of 1,948 on 304 lists). Before, each answer took its own figure and its own copy of the list.
- MathML's `<factorof/>`, a ∣ b: true where b is a whole multiple of a (0 ∣ 0 included), as JavaScript's `b % a === 0`, on whole numbers within ±2^53; drawn ∣ with the intent `divides`.
- MathML's `<equivalent/>` of two conditions, ⟺, as JavaScript's `===` of their truths; drawn with the intent `if-and-only-if`.
- LaTeX's everyday forms through LaTeXML: degrees, `\sin(30^\circ)`, computed as x · (π / 180); percent, `15\%`, as x / 100; a word as a subscript, `v_{\text{max}}` or `T_{room}`, as one name (`--v_max`, `--T_room`); `\bar{x}`, `\hat{p}` and `\tilde{y}` as one name, the letter and its mark (`--x̄`, `--p̂`); and `\prod` with limits. Each is drawn as typed. Of 70 inputs an author might type, 54 build (was 48).
- Conditions typed in words, as LaTeX writes them and LaTeXML reads them: `\text{if } x > 0`, `x > 0 \text{ and } y > 0`, `x < 0 \text{ or } x > 1` (which LaTeXML reads as x ⟨0 or x⟩ 1, angle brackets), `(x < 0) \lor (x > 1)`, with `and` binding before `or`, and `otherwise` or `else` as the last case. Any other word stops the build and names the words it takes; a ⟨…⟩ inside a sum stops it and says to put each relation in parentheses.
- LaTeX's chained conditions through LaTeXML, `0 \le a < 100` and `a \le b = c`. LaTeXML writes them as an `<and/>` whose relations after the first open with `<share/>`, the term before it; they are decided as JavaScript's `0 <= a && a < 100` and drawn as one chain. A `<share/>` anywhere else stops the build with its line; before, it read as an empty name.
- d3's quantile of a series at a p the page sets (`<ci>p</ci>`, or a formula of names): d3's min at p ≤ 0, its max at p ≥ 1, the interpolation between, as quantile.js gives them, tied zeros included. A p that is unset or not a number gives no answer. For 20 values the page sets it is 148 KB of CSS, and a change costs 19 ms in Chrome and 37 in WebKit (117 KB and 15 / 26 ms with p written in the page).
- `<in/>` and `<notin/>` in a formula's truth and in a `<piecewise>` condition: x in an `<interval>` (closed, open, open-closed or closed-open, either end ±∞), decided by JavaScript's `<=` and `<` on its ends, or x in ℤ, ℕ (with 0, as MathML defines it) or ℝ, decided by Number.isInteger and isFinite. LaTeX's `x \in [0, 1)` and `n \in \mathbb{N}` give the same through LaTeXML. ℚ is refused: every double is a rational number. They are drawn with ∈ and ∉, the interval's brackets and MathML's intents.
- `mathml.mjs` computes gcd and lcm of names the page sets on the page, exactly, as the build's integers give them: Euclid's algorithm written out 80 steps on CSS's `rem()`, which is exact on whole numbers below 2^53 in both engines, each remainder kept in three registered integers. 1920 / gcd(1920, 1080) is 16. An lcm past 2^53 is the double nearest it; a fraction, a value from 2^53 up or an unset name gives no answer. A change of one argument costs 3.7 ms in Chrome and 5 in WebKit; a gcd of two names is about 110 KB of CSS.
- `mathml.mjs` computes arcsec, arccsc, arcsech, arccsch and arccoth of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value, so every elementary function MathML has is now computed of a name. arcsec and arccsc go through arctan's core with √(x² − 1) below √2 and 1 over it above, so neither cancels; the other three are arccosh, arcsinh and arctanh of 1/x without rounding 1/x first: series of 1/x as a pair past 32, ln of an exact pair elsewhere, ln 2 − ln x below 2^-26. Outside each one's domain there's no answer. A change of x costs 8.6 to 10.7 ms in Chrome and 19 to 24 in Safari. A `<log/>` whose `<logbase>` the page sets is still refused; its message now says so instead of listing the functions.
- MathML's logic in a formula's truth and in a `<piecewise>` condition: `<and/>`, `<or/>`, `<xor/>`, `<not/>` and `<implies/>` of relations and of each other, decided as JavaScript's `&&`, `||` and `!` decide them (xor true for an odd count, implies as `!a || b`), with `<eq/>` as `===` and `<neq/>` as `!==` among them. A truth prints true or false; it's drawn with ∧, ∨, ⊻, ¬ and ⇒ and their intents, a logic inside another in parentheses. Where `<or/>`, `<xor/>`, `<not/>`, `<implies/>` and `<neq/>` stood as a formula they were refused.
- `mathml.mjs` computes arccot, sech, csch and coth of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: arccot as arctan's two branches the other way round, the others as 1 over cosh's, sinh's or tanh's pair, divided once and corrected. arccot of 0 has no answer (MathML's arccot is atan(1/x)), nor csch and coth of |x| below 2^-1022, nor sech and csch past 709. A change of x costs 5.8 ms (arccot, sech) to 10.2 (coth) in Chrome, 12 to 23 in WebKit.
- d3's `mode` of a series, `<csymbol definitionURL="https://d3js.org/d3-array/summarize#mode">mode</csymbol>` applied to the series' `<ci>`: the value that appears most often, the first to appear where two tie, as d3 picks it. MathML's `<mode/>` is refused: MathML doesn't say which value a tie gives.
- d3's `quantile` of a series: `<csymbol definitionURL="https://d3js.org/d3-array/summarize#quantile">quantile</csymbol>` applied to the series' `<ci>` and p, a `<cn>` (0.9 for the 90th percentile). Its answer is d3's double: between the values ranked ⌊(n − 1) · p⌋ and the next, the min at p 0 or below, the max at 1 or above.
- d3's `variance` and `deviation` of a series, as `sum` and `count` are written: `<csymbol definitionURL="https://d3js.org/d3-array/summarize#variance">variance</csymbol>` applied to the series' `<ci>`. Missing slots are skipped as d3 skips them, and the answer is d3's double, worked out by d3's own loop; there is none under two values. MathML's `<variance/>` and `<sdev/>` stay refused: MathML doesn't say whether they divide by n or n − 1.
- `<output data-dcss-result="months">` reads a series' `data-dcss-count` where no formula has that name: its number of rows, written as text, so a total can say what it covers ("285 across 2 of 6 months").
- An answer the build can work out (from numbers written in the page, in `dcss-format-fixed` with its settings written as numbers) is written into its output and its readers as text, `<data value="1200.5">1,200.5</data>`, the same text format.css prints: it shows with no CSS, in Firefox, in reader mode, in a feed and when copied. An answer that reads a number the page sets in CSS is printed by CSS as before.
- d3's `maxIndex` and `minIndex` of a series, as `sum` and `count` are written, and `<span data-dcss-label="best">no month yet</span>`, which shows the row header at that index ("May"), or its own text while no row wins; a tie goes to the first row, as in d3.
- A table can hold one series a column: `<th scope="col" data-dcss-series="y2025">2025</th>` in its header row names the column, each body row's cell under it is a slot (marked `data-dcss-value`, empty for a missing month), `<tfoot>` rows are left out for totals, and each row's `<th scope="row">` is its label. Merged cells and rows of another length are refused, so no value can stand in another column's place.
- d3's `sum` and `count` of a series, as MathML 4 writes a function defined elsewhere: `<csymbol definitionURL="https://d3js.org/d3-array/summarize#sum">sum</csymbol>` applied to the series' `<ci>`. A total skips missing slots as d3.sum does (0 with none present); a sum over i still has no answer while one slot is missing.
- `node mathml.mjs page.html built.html --watch` builds the page again each time it is saved; a refused save prints why and leaves the last good page.
- MathML's `<selector/>` reads a series' slot: `<apply><selector/><ci>sales</ci><ci>i</ci></apply>` is `sales_i`.
- A series' cell can be HTML's `<data value="1200">1,200</data>`: the number in `value`, the text as the page shows it, so a table keeps its thousands separators, percent signs and "n/a" (an empty value is missing).
- `mathml.mjs` reads a page's data: `data-dcss-series` on a table or a list, one `data-dcss-value` cell an item (its own number, `var(--name)` for one the page sets, or empty for a missing one), and `data-dcss-count` for the number of items. `<mean/>`, `<min/>`, `<max/>` and `<median/>` of the series give d3's answer, missing items skipped; `sales_i` in a sum reads item i.
- `data-dcss-scope` marks the element where a page's numbers and formulas live: each result is worked out once there, and `<output data-dcss-result="name">`, the page's own CSS as `var(--name)` and `var(--name-ready)`, `data-dcss-error="name"` (a message shown while the result has no answer) and later formulas read it anywhere inside.
- A formula beside text in a paragraph builds into the sentence, where it was refused.
- `toDcss()` returns a report (scopes, inputs, series, each result and when it has no answer), and `node mathml.mjs page.html built.html --report report.json` writes it.
- `mathml.mjs` computes arcsinh, arccosh and arctanh of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: series near their zeros (odd ones below 2^-5, arccosh as √(2t) times a series within 2^-10 of 1), elsewhere ln of an exact pair (x + √(x² ± 1), or (1 + x)/(1 − x) halved) by the exp correction. Fisher's z of a correlation, arctanh(r), now builds. None of 60,000 seeded x wrong. A change of x costs 7.8 to 9.0 ms in Chrome, 18 to 20 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes sinh, cosh and tanh of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: the exact exp's pair Z = e^|x|, its reciprocal as a pair, Z ∓ it; sinh's series below 2^-5 (x³/6 as a pair); tanh as the corrected quotient. tanh past 20 is ±1; sinh and cosh past 709 have no answer. None of 60,000 seeded x wrong. A change of x costs 5.1 ms (cosh) to 9.1 (tanh) in Chrome, 12 to 21 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes a root of whole degree 3 to 1,000 (other than 4, 8…) of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true root: x scaled by 2^(−nk) near 1, CSS's `pow()` as a guess, y^n as a pair by squaring, one Newton step and the halfway test. ∛64 is 4 and ∛−8 is −2; none of 140,000 seeded x wrong. A cube root costs 5.1 ms a change in Chrome, 8 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes sec and csc of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true 1/cos x and 1/sin x: the cosine's or sine's pair, then one division by CSS as a guess corrected by TwoProduct, the margin carried relative. sec(94π/2), 1.6e16, is exact; none of 200,000 seeded x wrong. 7.2 ms a change in Chrome, 14 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` takes LaTeXML's Content MathML for `\begin{cases}`, `\log_{10}`, `\sin^2 x`, `\sin^{-1} x`, `\bmod`, `\cdot`, decimals (`<cn type="float">`) and `\phi`: 34 computing formulas typed in LaTeX and converted by latexmlmath build and give the exact double (probes/dcss/latex-check.mjs). ARQMath, converted by LaTeXML: 476 of 1,164 formulas compile.
- `mathml.mjs` computes tan and cot of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: sin's reduction, tan(iπ/512) for an eighth of a turn from a table, tan x = −1/tan(x − π/2) for the rest, tan r as a pair, one corrected division of pairs. 200,000 of 200,000 in the model, 92 of 92 in both engines; 7.6 ms a change in Chrome, 15 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes arcsin and arccos of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: √(1 − x²) as a pair, the smaller of |x| and it over the larger as a pair, arctan's table and series, then π/2 − it or not so that neither cancels near ±1. 200,000 of 200,000 in the model, 92 of 92 in both engines. The whole great-circle distance now computes on the page: 35 of 35 textbook formulas. 9.4 ms a change in Chrome, 18 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes arctan of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: 1/x past 1 as a pair (CSS's division as a guess, corrected by TwoProduct), c = j/256 nearest, u = (t − c)/(1 + t c) as a pair, atan(j/256) from a table, the series to u¹¹; arctan(±0) is ±0. 200,000 of 200,000 seeded x exact in the model, 68 of 68 in both engines. 6.6 ms a change in Chrome, 13 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes sin and cos of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value, for |x| up to 2^20: k = round(x · 512/π), x − kπ/512 in four exact parts, sin and cos of a quarter turn's 257 steps as pairs, the series to r⁷ with its first three terms as pairs. sin(29π) and sin(−0) are exact; none of 200,000 seeded x wrong. The haversine and a projectile's range now build: all 34 textbook formulas. 8.1 ms a change in Chrome, 16 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` computes ln, and log to any base it knows (10, 2, 3…; 1/ln b as a pair), of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: CSS's `log()` as a guess (any platform's), δ = x · e^(−guess) − 1 from the exact exp's pair, ln x = guess + δ − δ²/2; within 2^-10 of 1 a series of x − 1. No answer for x a few ulps from 1 that a pair can't decide (none in 80,000 ratios and rates of data). Sound level, pH and log returns now build: 32 of 33 textbook formulas. 6.9 ms a change in Chrome, 13 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` takes LaTeXML's \binom: C(n, k) exactly where the build knows n and k, and where the page sets them, n! / (k! (n − k)!) from the factorial table rounded to a whole number, exact below 2^50 (no answer above, or past n = 170).
- `mathml.mjs` takes `<factorial/>`, `<gcd/>` and `<lcm/>`: n! for whole n from 0 to 170 is the double nearest it, worked out when the page is built or picked on the page from the 171 when the page sets n (any other n, no answer); gcd and lcm of whole numbers the build knows, exactly. A value the build works out from 3.4e38 up is written as m · 2^e, which Chrome doesn't clamp, so 170! no longer stops the build.
- `mathml.mjs` computes exp of a name the page sets on the page, to the double nearest the true value: e^x as a pair of doubles carried to about 2^-76 (Cody-Waite reduction, a 64-entry table, a degree-8 polynomial, TwoSum and TwoProduct, each step fenced and exact in both engines), shown rounded unless it lies too near halfway (about 3 in a million) or x is outside −708 to 709. P·e^(rt), the normal density and logistic growth now build: 27 of 29 textbook formulas. About 68 KB of style each; 3.7 ms a change in Chrome, 8 in WebKit.
- `mathml.mjs` takes a root of any whole degree of a number it knows, the double nearest the true root: ∛64 is 4 and ∛−8 is −2 (`Math.pow(64, 1 / 3)` is 3.9999999999999996). Of a name the page sets, degrees other than powers of 2 still stop the build.
- `mathml.mjs` takes a sum or product to a bound the page sets, written with its ceiling, min(n, 100): the terms are written out to 100, each counting while i ≤ n, as JavaScript's loop; a term past n is never read (its names unset, NaN or infinite change nothing). Up to 100 terms: 4 ms of style in Chrome and 9 in WebKit for each change of n.
- `mathml.mjs` computes `<exp/>`, `<ln/>`, `<log/>` (base 10 or a `<logbase>`), the trigonometric and hyperbolic functions and their inverses, OpenMath's `transc1`, and e to a power, of numbers the build knows, with Node's `Math` when the page is built, and writes the double in: P·e^(0.05·10) beside a name the page sets. Of a name the page sets they still stop the build. LaTeXML's 𝑒 reads the page's `--e` as 𝜋 reads `--π` (no answer before where the page set none); a value worked out through them holds while the page sets neither, is the page's e to that power where it sets `--e`, and no answer where it sets `--π`. 429 of ARQMath's 1,164 formulas with an operator compile, 5 more.
- `mathml.mjs` takes `<quotient/>` and `<rem/>`, MathML's integer division, as Math.trunc(a / b) and a % b (−0 included) for whole numbers within ±2^26; a fraction, a divisor of 0 or a larger number leaves no answer. Hours and minutes, cents, weeks and days.
- `mathml.mjs` takes sums and products over an index (MathML's `<sum/>` and `<product/>` with `<bvar>` and limits, LaTeX's `\sum_{i=1}^{n}` through LaTeXML), written out when the page is built and added as JavaScript's loop adds them: weighted sums, a variance with n − 1 written out, a net present value. The bounds are whole numbers the build knows; x_i reads `--x_1`, `--x_2`….
- `mathml.mjs` takes `<piecewise>`: the first `<piece>` whose condition holds, else `<otherwise>`, as JavaScript's if/else chain (tax brackets, tiered prices, guards). A piece not chosen changes nothing, even where it is NaN or infinite: each piece's value travels in four registered integers, whose NaN is 0.
- `mathml.mjs` takes π and e as numbers: `<pi/>`, `<exponentiale/>` and OpenMath's `nums1` symbols are Math.PI and Math.E, and LaTeXML's 𝜋 reads the page's `--π` where it sets one, Math.PI where it doesn't (no answer before, unless the page set it). e to a power stops the build: it is exp(x), which CSS doesn't compute to JavaScript's double, and Math.pow(Math.E, x) differs from Math.exp(x) for 92 of 100 x (16 ARQMath formulas compiled it as pow(--e, x)).
- `mathml.mjs` takes LaTeXML's square brackets around one thing as grouping, drawn as brackets: 440 of ARQMath's 1,164 formulas with an operator compile, 6 more.
- `.dcss-median-index` and `.dcss-quantile-index` take missing items as d3 does: an `<li>` with no `--value` counts in n and is never the answer, so past the numbers the index is the largest one's, -1 with none (d3's `quantileIndex([1, 1, 1, null, 2, 3, 3, 3], 0.5)` is 4). They gave no answer before.

- `.dcss-bin`, d3.bin() on the grid with a domain and thresholds, or `--count` for d3's tick thresholds: one `<li>` per bin in a second list, each reading its count, x0 and x1.
- `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation`, d3.variance() and d3.deviation() on the grid: each value's square as two exact halves, read through two anchors, so 64 values in 64ths to ±1024 give the true variance rounded once.
- `.dcss-median-index` and `.dcss-quantile-index` on the grid, written as the median is: the list sorted once, the index of the value at k read as the list's height. 0 to 64 values within ±131,072; with ties, the copy the order picks.

- CSS functions where the browser has `@function`: every easing (`--dcss-ease-cubic-in-out(t)`) and two-stop linear, pow, sqrt and log scales (`--dcss-scale-linear(x, d0, d1, r0, r1)`).

- License headers (SPDX) on every sheet; `package.json` for npm.

### Changed

- `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation` read through two elements of class `dcss-read` and the page's own element inside them, in place of three plain `<div>`s.

- A call's answers (`--dcss-sum`, `--dcss-count`, `--dcss-length`, `--dcss-mean`, `--dcss-min`, `--dcss-max`, `--dcss-variance`, `--dcss-deviation`) reach the elements directly inside the element that reads them, not their descendants: set `--value: var(--dcss-mean)` on such an element to pass one deeper. A printed summary moving every frame costs 10 ms a frame in Chrome instead of 24.
- `.dcss-format-fixed` and `.dcss-format-percent` compute on their `::after`.
- `.dcss-format-fixed` and `.dcss-format-percent` take a short path where the rounded number is under 10^6 and not near a half: about 20 of the `::after`'s 63 properties worked out, the text still d3's. 60 outputs changing cost 9.3 ms a change in Chrome instead of 21.0, 13.4 in Safari's WebKit instead of 19.4; WebKit takes it only on a page that is restyling, a still page the long path. Twenty summaries printing their mean, variance and max, a value moving: 21.3 ms a frame in Chrome instead of 33.1, 40.2 in WebKit instead of 63.5.
- `.dcss-summarize`, `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation` are one figure laid out once: its items sorted, so it gives the median too (`--dcss-median`), and three nested readers placed by grid area, with no anchor in WebKit. A list with a fraction has no answer once its values, 1024 more each, pass 2^18 between them (was: its negatives 1024 more). Twenty printed summaries, a value moving: 8.3 ms a frame in Chrome, 12 in Safari's WebKit; variance 8.2, 10.3 (was 10.8, 13.6); mean, variance and max printed from one figure 21.3 and 40.2 (was 41.6 and 74.7, a figure each), with the format's short path below.
- The reductions' reader (`.dcss-sum` to `.dcss-extent`) is placed out of flow: a printed mean moving every frame costs 11 ms a frame in Chrome instead of 41.
- The package is `dcss` and its repository noscripting/dcss (were `dcss-array.css` and noscripting/dcss-array), and its sheets keep their own names: `dcss.css`, `dcss-all.css` and `dcss-extras.css` (were `dcss-array.css`, `dcss-array-all.css` and `dcss-array-extras.css`).
- The median, quantile, quantileSorted, medianIndex, quantileIndex, variance and deviation calls read their layout one element above what prints them, so a formatter on the answer is no longer restyled in each container pass. For 20 calls of 8 values with one moving every frame, printed by `dcss-format-fixed`, Chrome's time a frame goes from 5.1 to 3.4 ms for medianIndex and from 33 to 22 ms for variance (29 to 37 fps); WebKit's is unchanged, and calls nothing reads cost Chrome about 1 ms more; the figure's markup and the answers are unchanged. A variance figure now takes `contain: layout`.
- Formulas are drawn with the concept names of MathML's intent core list wherever it has the concept: `divided-by` (was `divide`), `less-than-or-equal-to` and `greater-than-or-equal-to` (were without `-to`), `not-equal-to` (was `not-equals`), `square-root-of`, `logarithm`, `natural-logarithm`, `exponential`, `sine` to `arc-hyperbolic-cotangent` (were `sin` to `arccoth`), `remainder`, `binomial-coefficient`, `defined-as` (was `assign`), `greatest-common-divisor` and `least-common-multiple` (were `gcd` and `lcm`), as the Math Working Group's intent core list names them.

- An answer that prints no number says so: a formula's output and each `data-dcss-result` element show a dash, or the words written inside the element (`<output data-dcss-result="total">not yet</output>`), while there is no answer or its format can't print it, and with no CSS. An element holding text was refused; one holding a digit still is. An answer the build knows hides its words and its `data-dcss-error` message with the `hidden` attribute, so they don't show with no CSS either.
- The build's answers are no live regions: each `<output>` it writes, and each `<output data-dcss-result>` with no `aria-live` or `role` of its own, is `aria-live="off"`, so a screen reader doesn't announce every number a change of input moves; inside an `aria-live` region of the page's own it is `role="none"`, part of the region's text. A printed cell is a `<span>`, as the cell's own text would be.
- A formula's answer ("= 118.5") is set in the font the browser draws the formula in (`font-family: math`), so the formula and its answer read as one, in a figure and in a sentence; the page's own CSS overrides it.
- README.md and TUTORIAL.md start from the MathML build: a table, a formula and one command; the classes come after, without the build.
- `mathml.mjs`'s live functions skip the finiteness check on their intermediate steps (bounded, and x's own domain flag catches a NaN or infinite x; the answer keeps it): 10 to 17% fewer tokens. A change of x, alone on a page, Chrome / WebKit: exp 2.3 / 4 ms, ln 3.8 / 8, sin 6.0 / 12, tan 5.6 / 12, arctan 5.7 / 12, arcsin 6.7 / 14.
- `mathml.mjs` computes exp, ln, log and the trigonometric and hyperbolic functions of numbers it knows to the double nearest the true value, rounded once, in BigInt at two precisions that must agree (Python's decimal agrees on 29,000 of 29,000), instead of Node's `Math`: Node's differed in the last bit for 227 of 2,000 exp arguments and 527 of sinh. Chrome's own `Math` gives the same double for exp, log, log10, log2, sin, cos, tan, asin and acos and cbrt (100,000 of 100,000).
- `.dcss-format-si` rounds once, exactly, with no tables: 20 outputs moving every frame took 61 to 75 ms of rendering a frame in Safari, now 14 (Chrome 1.3 to 0.9), and `families/format.css` is 135 KB (237). Every digit is still d3's, including the 527 doubles that come closest to a tie past 10^22, now in the check.
- `.dcss-format-general` runs on the fixed format's exact rounding (d3's general notation is toPrecision, which rounds as toFixed does at its digits less one less the exponent): 20 outputs moving every frame took 70 ms of rendering a frame in Safari, now 11. It also prints a value that rounds up to 0.000001, which it left blank.
- The sum, mean, min, max and extent no longer round a value off the 1/64 grid to the nearest 64th: it leaves no answer, `--dcss-err` 1, as the median and variance already did (12.3 and 7.85 summed to 20.140625). An item with no number is skipped quietly by them too (`--dcss-err` 0, their `<output>` printing the answer), and a value past a call's range is error 1, not 2.
- Every call measures across and down whatever the page's `writing-mode` (in `vertical-rl` a median read -64,895.5).
- The median skips an item with no number, as d3 does, and so does the quantile at `--p` 0, 0.5 and 1; quantileSorted answers unless it reads one. Compiled, the median and the quantile at any `--p` skip it too (the compiler drops it from the literal data). Each item is laid out twice and a missing one sends a copy to each end, so the middle of the copies is the middle of the numbers. Their 1/64 steps now end at 131,071 (whole numbers still reach 8,257,535).
- Lists (`.list`, `.pager`), loops (`.loop-*`) and sheets (`.sheet` and its folds and formats) moved out of `dcss.css` into `dcss-extras.css`: their names have no `dcss-` prefix, so a page's own `.list` or `.sum` no longer gets their rules. A page that uses them links `dcss-extras.css`.
- The reductions take signed numbers. A sum lays its positives out across and its negatives down, so it takes any, with no offset. A mean takes values from -1024, each negative one offset by 1024; min, max and extent take values within ±131,072, or whole numbers to 8,257,535. An empty `<output>` prints d3.format's minus (U+2212). The sum's list no longer gives the count, mean and length, since its height holds the negatives: a mean's list gives the sum, count and length. A sum says nothing of an item it skips (d3 doesn't either).
- The sheet's own names (its grids, readers and internal numbers, `arrays-*` before) are `dcss-*`; the 40 that a call also used have names of their own (`.dcss-width-sum`, `--dcss-array-length`; the list is `src/names.mjs`'s `RENAMED`).

- The list median, quantile, quantileSorted and quantile scale take 1/64 steps within ±131,072 and whole numbers up to 8,257,535 (was ±1024).
- Stereographic points update about ten times faster, and mercator and equirectangular points a fifth faster.
- A median, quantile or quantileSorted of a list takes the list once, with three plain nested elements after it, in place of a second copy of the list inside `.dcss-order-upper`.
- A quantile scale takes its list once, with four plain nested elements after it, in place of three copies of the list and nine wrappers. It updates faster: a query change on 20 scales of 64 values took 115 ms in Chrome and 159 in Safari (272 and 265 before), one value changed 18 and 36 ms (154 and 206).
- Threshold and quantize scales take no list and no wrappers: a threshold scale's thresholds are `--domain-0` to `--domain-15`, a quantize scale's come from its domain, and everything inside the call reads the answer. A query change on 20 scales of 16 thresholds took 3.1 ms in Chrome and 4 in Safari for threshold (8.6 and 14 with a list), 4.4 and 7 for quantize (16.3 and 27). Thresholds out of order or with a gap are refused.
- Cumsum's second list needs no class and its items no wrapper: everything inside the i-th item reads the i-th running total.
- A scale takes two stops unless it has the class `dcss-piecewise`: `--stops` and the stops past the second need it now. Two-stop scales update about four times faster.
- A list's length is counted in 8 `:has()` rules, one a binary digit, in place of one a length (65 for a median, 512 for the identity scale's ticks): 50 medians of 64 show in 51 ms in Chrome and 88 in Safari (62 and 134 before); `families/array.css` 431 to 394 KB, `ticks.css` 172 to 128 KB.

### Removed

- The typed calls (`families/aggregate.css`, `order.css` and `extrema.css`, and `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation` in `array.css`): values given as `--type-N` and `--value-N` on one element have no boxes to lay out, so each call was an arithmetic machine emulating d3's doubles, nulls, strings and objects, 5.6 MB in all. Lists on the grid are the one data model: sum, count, mean, cumsum, min, max, extent, the index extrema, median, quantile and quantileSorted take a list. Gone with them: fsum, fcumsum, mode, scan, variance and deviation, and values that aren't numbers on the grid. `families/` is 1.3 MB in all (86 KB gzipped), `array.css` 393 to 149 KB.
- Colors (`families/color.css`, `color-text.css`): gamutant.css reads a CSS color's channels as numbers, and CSS's `color-mix()` and relative colors convert and interpolate them.
- Date parsing (`families/date-parse.css`, `date-parse-local.css`): its text came as character codes, and the program that writes the codes holds the date as a number.
- Pacific time (`.dcss-local-*` intervals and `.dcss-local-date-format-N`): one fixed zone, America/Los_Angeles, that d3's tests use. Time is UTC: `time.css` 1.6 MB to 315 KB, `date-format.css` 85 to 36 KB.
- `.dcss-pack-five` (d3.packSiblings of five circles): `hierarchy.css` 2.55 MB to 6.5 KB.
- `.dcss-geo-mercator-fit-extent` and `.dcss-geo-transverse-mercator-fit-extent`: `geo.css` 1.6 MB to 248 KB.
- `.dcss-random-lcg` (`families/random.css`).
- `.dcss-call-input`: a call takes another's answer written straight inside it. A call with no answer publishes no number, so the next fails too and prints nothing.
- The markup that wrote a list two or three times, and the named wrappers: `.dcss-call-read`, `.dcss-call-result`, `.dcss-range-copy`, `.dcss-order-upper`, `.dcss-source-order`, `.dcss-quantile-low`, `.dcss-quantile-high` and `.dcss-scan-results`, and a threshold or quantize scale's list. Every call takes its data once, read through plain elements or none; a page with those classes gets no answer, and the compiler refuses them. `families/array.css` is 24 KB smaller (431 KB), and its parts shrink with it: the median part is 2.4 KB gzipped (4.2 before).

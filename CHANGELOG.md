# Changelog

All notable changes to dcss are written here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html): a class, an input, an output, a function or a file removed or changed in meaning is a major version.

## [Unreleased]

## [0.0.1] - 2026-11-04

The first release: d3's modules computed by CSS layout, checked against d3 in Chrome, WebKit and Firefox (SPEC.md's report: what is right, not covered, a gap and wrong, per call).

### Added
- The compiler's cumsum (`pipeline/scan.mjs`) takes a list in decimals: a value and a prefix are stored as whole numbers of the list's grid, and the authored path hands the sheet its `--decimals`.
- A value written with a dot takes its list in two decimals, with no setting: the order and index calls, the quantile scale, a mean, min or extent, the summary, variance and deviation, rollups, lookups, shares and cumsum; `--decimals` for more, up to 6. A median of 19.99, 5.49 and 12 is 12; a rollup of 19.99 and 5.49 is 25.48; a share of 19.99 among 5.49 and 12 is 0.5334.
- Date formats by d3's letter: `.dcss-date-format-Y` prints `%Y`, `.dcss-date-format-B` `%B`, `.dcss-date-format-_S` `%_S`, beside the numbered classes, which stay.
- `--find` on `.dcss-sum`, `.dcss-count`, `.dcss-length`, `.dcss-mean`, `.dcss-min`, `.dcss-max` and `.dcss-extent`: only the rows whose `--key` equals it, a spreadsheet's SUMIF, COUNTIF, AVERAGEIF, MINIFS and MAXIFS.
- Numbers in the page's language: a format in an element with `lang="de-DE"` (or any of d3-format's 58 locales but `en-IN`) prints as d3's `formatLocale` does, `1.234,50 €`; its currency with `--symbol: 1`. A language d3 has no file for prints as before.
- `.dcss-lookup`: a spreadsheet's exact lookup. The `--value` of the row whose `--key` is the figure's `--find`, as `--dcss-lookup`, as d3.index's get gives it; a missing key is error 2, a key in two rows error 1; no limit on rows.
- `.dcss-evaluate`: your formula's value at one number, `--at`, as `--dcss-evaluate` inside the call, with its slope `--dcss-slope` beside it: a label on a curve, a point to draw a tangent from.
- `steps.mjs`, the page build in Node: `node steps.mjs page.html built.html` writes a solve's nested `<i>`s from `data-dcss-steps` or `data-dcss-within`, a `.dcss-solve-every`'s pieces, a `.dcss-evaluate`'s `<i>` and a Newton solve's `--dcss-slope`; it was part of `mathml.mjs`.
- `.dcss-solve-every`: every place the formula reaches `--target` between `--from` and `--to`, not just one. The build cuts the range into pieces (`data-dcss-pieces`, or `data-dcss-apart`, the closest two answers may be) and copies what the call holds into each, so each answer gets its own dot or label; a piece with none hides its copy. With `.dcss-solve-turning-point`, every peak and dip.
- `.dcss-sample` with `--columns`: a formula of two inputs on a grid, `--dcss-input` across from `--from` to `--to` and `--dcss-input-y` down from `--from-y` to `--to-y`, for a heat map, or with `.dcss-sum` the volume under it.
- A math call's ends (`--from`, `--to`, a solve's `--target` and `--guess`, a solve-rows' `--top` and `--bottom`) are read only as set on the call itself; a value from an element around it, such as a chart mark's `--from`, gives `--dcss-err` 1 instead of an answer on the wrong range.

- `.dcss-solve-turning-point` with `.dcss-solve`: where the page's own formula is highest or lowest between `--from` and `--to`, from the slope the build writes.
- `data-dcss-within="0.002"` on a `.dcss-solve`: say how close the answer must be, in the formula's own units, and the build counts the tries and writes them, the fewest that reach it.
- The build writes a formula's slope: on a page with a `.dcss-solve-newton`, `node steps.mjs page.html built.html` writes `--dcss-slope`, the derivative of each `--dcss-formula` in the page's `<style>`s, beside it, so Newton's steps need only the formula. A slope the page writes is left as it is; a formula it can't differentiate gets none and is named with its line, and the rest of the page builds.
- `.dcss-sample`, beside a list call: each item's value is the page's own formula (`--dcss-formula` from `--dcss-input`, as for `.dcss-solve` and `.dcss-solve-rows`) at the item's middle between `--from` and `--to`, so `.dcss-sum` gives the area under a curve, `.dcss-max` its highest value, `.dcss-mean` its average. One rule can feed a solve, an invert and a sample at once.
- `data-dcss-steps="13"` on a `.dcss-solve` or a `.dcss-solve-rows`: the build (`node steps.mjs page.html built.html`) writes its nested `<i>`s or its pairs, so a page never counts them; the built page has no script and is what a hand-written one would be.
- `.dcss-motion`, in a family of its own, `families/motion.css`, on store.css's frame cell: the page writes an acceleration once (`--dcss-motion-acceleration` from `--dcss-motion-position` and `--dcss-motion-speed`) and the element takes a leapfrog step a frame, so a pendulum, a spring or a pull toward the pointer moves by its own equation, keeping its swing for minutes.
- `.dcss-solve-rows`, in families/math.css: the inverse of the page's own formula on every row of a box at once, in one layout. The page writes its formula once (`--dcss-formula` from `--dcss-input`) on pairs of `<i>`s, `--from` and `--to` across the box and `--top` and `--bottom` down it, and each line starts where the formula reaches its value, so what starts the line sits on the answer: 24 moons on one orbit, the ticks of an eased axis. Within half a pixel on a 1,000 px box at 32 to 64 pairs.
- `.dcss-solve-newton`, with `.dcss-solve`: give the formula's slope `--dcss-slope` beside it, and `--guess` if you have one, and each `<i>` after the two ends is one of Newton's steps, kept inside the bracket. Kepler's equation to a double's last digit in 8 to 10 `<i>`s where halving takes 54, at about half the cost a frame.
- Which input gives a value, in a new family, `families/math.css`: `.dcss-solve` finds where the page's own formula (`--dcss-formula` from `--dcss-input`, written once) reaches `--target` (0 without one) between `--from` and `--to`, halving over nested `<i>`s, to `--dcss-solve` and `--dcss-solve-width`: a spreadsheet's Goal Seek. d3-geo's naturalEarth1Raw and equalEarthRaw, forward in `families/geo.css` and inverted on it.
- The CSS functions are a sheet of their own, `families/functions.css`, and one file per call in `parts/functions/`; the family sheets and parts hold the classes only, as before the functions (the Mercator part 17.7 KB again, not 29.2).
- CSS functions for the UTC intervals where the browser has `@function`: `--dcss-utc-<unit>-floor(t)`, `-ceil(t)`, `-round(t)`, `-offset(t, step)` and `-count(start, stop)` for day, hour, minute, second and each weekday's week, equal to d3-time to the millisecond.
- A format class on an element that holds outputs formats each output inside it that has no class of its own, with `--precision` and the other settings written once on that element.
- `.dcss-summarize`: one list, written once, gives its sum, count, length, mean, min, max, median, variance and deviation (`--dcss-sum` to `--dcss-deviation`). Every element after the list, and everything inside it, gets every answer (an `<output>` alone, or a `<p>` of several), each such element on a line of its own, with no element added. Up to 64 items in 64ths from -1024 to 65535 (±1024 for the variance); each answer is to the bit in Chrome and Safari (1,836 of 1,836 on 304 lists). Release Firefox has no view timelines and gets few. Before, each answer took its own figure and its own copy of the list.
- `.dcss-median-index` and `.dcss-quantile-index` take missing items as d3 does: an `<li>` with no `--value` counts in n and is never the answer, so past the numbers the index is the largest one's, -1 with none (d3's `quantileIndex([1, 1, 1, null, 2, 3, 3, 3], 0.5)` is 4). They gave no answer before.
- `.dcss-bin`, d3.bin() on the grid with a domain and thresholds, or `--count` for d3's tick thresholds: one `<li>` per bin in a second list, each reading its count, x0 and x1.
- `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation`, d3.variance() and d3.deviation() on the grid: each value's square as two exact halves, read through two anchors, so 64 values in 64ths to ±1024 give the true variance rounded once.
- `.dcss-median-index` and `.dcss-quantile-index` on the grid, written as the median is: the list sorted once, the index of the value at k read as the list's height. 0 to 64 values within ±131,072; with ties, the copy the order picks.
- One file per public class in `parts/` (`parts/dcss-median.css`), each its family's rules cut to that call, usable without `dcss.css`.
- CSS functions where the browser has `@function`: two-stop linear, pow, sqrt and log scales (`--dcss-scale-linear(x, d0, d1, r0, r1)`).
- ISO date formats in the date parsers: `%Y-%m-%d`, `%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ` and `%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S.%LZ`.
- License headers (SPDX) on every sheet; `package.json` for npm.

### Changed
- Every check expects the errors its SPEC.md row states as not covered (a value past the stated bounds, a string where d3 orders text, more items than stated, more decimals than the list takes, an engine's stated gap) and fails only on a gap or a wrong number; SPEC.md's report says, per row, what is right, not covered, a gap and wrong.

- The precision rule (DCSS.md, rule 6): a number is right to the pixel and the printed digits, never to the bit; in Firefox, whose `calc()` keeps 32-bit floats, right to the float it reads, within the float's steps. A call that cannot answer what a page writes is a gap, counted in SPEC.md's report.
- `.dcss-lookup` reaches ±8,388,607 (±83,886.07 in two decimals), from ±131,071; in Firefox ±2,097,151 and ±20,971.51, as far as it reads a container's width exactly.
- `.dcss-share` takes values and totals to 16,777,215 in the list's unit (Firefox 4,194,303), from whole numbers to 4,095; an item with no number takes no room, as d3's sum skips it, and its own share is error 2 (the whole list was error 1).
- `.dcss-cumsum` runs its totals to 8,388,607 in the list's unit, each value from 1 - 8,388,608 / n for n values (in Firefox a quarter of each), from ±1024 values and 262,143 px of running width.
- Firefox: a mean, min or extent of a list in decimals answers where an item like 1234.56 was error 1 (its grid test is within the float's steps, not an equality its division fails); a sum and a max there still take whole numbers.
- Firefox: a rollup key's positives or negatives read to 1,769,471 (17,694.71 in two decimals), from 262,143; a median, quantile or quantileSorted reads to 1,572,863 px from 524,287, interpolates in its floats where its whole-number ratio passes 2^24, and takes an item's decimals within its float's steps; the index calls and the quantile scale take a list in decimals there, where any was error 1; the quantile scale's layout reaches 2,031,615 px there (lo + 1,900,543), from 524,287.
- A page of many sums, counts, means, mins, maxes or extents loads in time linear in its calls in WebKit: 1,024 calls of 10 items in 1.3 s, 13.6 s before. The reductions read their list by the figure's own scroll range, `scroll(self)`, and name no timeline.
- A sum, count, length or mean restyles one item when one item changes, not its whole list: one item of 1,000 changed costs 1.2 ms of style in WebKit (was 38.4) and 0.5 in Chrome (was 3.4). The items no longer use `sibling-index()`; what an item holds (text, elements) still takes no room.
- Pages with many list calls style about 5 times faster in Chrome and 1.6 times in WebKit: the elements after a list are matched by their parent, not by a walk over the list's siblings (70 figures of 67,543 items: 19.4 s to 3.9 s in Chrome, 19.5 s to 12.0 s in WebKit).
- README.md and TUTORIAL.md start with no build: a list in a figure, its sum and mean printed; the tutorial builds a sales summary, a bar and a switch between years from the classes alone (`tutorial-check.mjs`).
- Six families where there were twelve: d3-array's ticks and nice (`.dcss-ticks`, `.dcss-nice`) are in `families/array.css`, where `families/ticks.css` held them; UTC time and date formats are one family, `families/dates.css` (was `time.css` and `date-format.css`); `.dcss-motion` has its own, `families/motion.css`.
- `.dcss-cumsum` takes up to 1,024 items (was 64): each running total is d3's, or has no answer (error 1) once its running width passes 262,143 px.
- `.dcss-summarize`, `.dcss-median`, `.dcss-quantile`, `.dcss-quantile-sorted`, `.dcss-median-index`, `.dcss-quantile-index` and `.dcss-scale-quantile` take lists of up to 1,024 items (was 64); a summary whose columns run past its reads has no answer (error 1) rather than a wrong one, and its variance and deviation stay at 64 items.
- `.dcss-summarize`, `.dcss-variance`, `.dcss-deviation`, `.dcss-median`, `.dcss-quantile`, `.dcss-quantile-sorted` and `.dcss-scale-quantile`: the figure is a plain block, the list out of its flow, with no limit on how many elements follow it; what follows the list is laid out as written, each element a block unless the page says otherwise, so outputs can share a line in a sentence.
- `.dcss-sum`, `.dcss-count`, `.dcss-mean`, `.dcss-min`, `.dcss-max` and `.dcss-extent` give their answer to each element after the list, everything inside it and their `::before` and `::after`, read by the figure (was the element after the list's own children); each is a block in flow, a `<figcaption>` stays where it is written, and the figure grows to hold them (no `height` needed). `.dcss-median-index` and `.dcss-quantile-index` lay out what follows the list the same way. A sum's or mean's list of 16,777,215 px or more (was 2^24) has no answer, error 1. In a list of whole numbers, an item with a style attribute but no number now voids a min, max, extent or mean (error 1).
- `.dcss-median`, `.dcss-quantile`, `.dcss-quantile-sorted`, `.dcss-median-index`, `.dcss-quantile-index` and `.dcss-scale-quantile` give their answer to each element after the list and everything inside it, as `.dcss-summarize` does, in place of three or four plain `<div>`s. A list with a value written with a point or a function takes 1/64 steps within ±65,536 (was ±131,072); whole-number lists keep -131,072 to 8,257,535.
- `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation` are read by the page's own element after the list, in place of three plain `<div>`s. A list with a fraction now takes values whose sum, each 1025 more, is under 132,735 (it was 2^18).

- A call's answers (`--dcss-sum`, `--dcss-count`, `--dcss-length`, `--dcss-mean`, `--dcss-min`, `--dcss-max`, `--dcss-variance`, `--dcss-deviation`) reach the elements directly inside the element that reads them, not their descendants: set `--value: var(--dcss-mean)` on such an element to pass one deeper. A printed summary moving every frame costs 10 ms a frame in Chrome instead of 24.
- `.dcss-format-fixed` and `.dcss-format-percent` compute on their `::after`.
- `.dcss-format-fixed` and `.dcss-format-percent` take a short path where the rounded number is under 10^6 and not near a half: about 20 of the `::after`'s 63 properties worked out, the text still d3's. 60 outputs changing cost 9.3 ms a change in Chrome instead of 21.0, 13.4 in Safari's WebKit instead of 19.4; WebKit takes it only on a page that is restyling, a still page the long path. Twenty summaries printing their mean, variance and max, a value moving: 21.3 ms a frame in Chrome instead of 33.1, 40.2 in WebKit instead of 63.5.
- `.dcss-summarize`, `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation` are one figure laid out once: its items sorted, so it gives the median too (`--dcss-median`), and three nested readers placed by grid area, with no anchor in WebKit. A list with a fraction has no answer once its values, 1024 more each, pass 2^18 between them (was: its negatives 1024 more). Twenty printed summaries, a value moving: 8.3 ms a frame in Chrome, 12 in Safari's WebKit; variance 8.2, 10.3 (was 10.8, 13.6); mean, variance and max printed from one figure 21.3 and 40.2 (was 41.6 and 74.7, a figure each), with the format's short path below.
- The reductions' reader (`.dcss-sum` to `.dcss-extent`) is placed out of flow: a printed mean moving every frame costs 11 ms a frame in Chrome instead of 41.
- The package is `dcss` and its repository noscripting/dcss (were `dcss-array.css` and noscripting/dcss-array), and its sheets keep their own names: `dcss.css`, `dcss-all.css` and `dcss-extras.css` (were `dcss-array.css`, `dcss-array-all.css` and `dcss-array-extras.css`).
- The median, quantile, quantileSorted, medianIndex, quantileIndex, variance and deviation calls read their layout one element above what prints them, so a formatter on the answer is no longer restyled in each container pass. For 20 calls of 8 values with one moving every frame, printed by `dcss-format-fixed`, Chrome's time a frame goes from 5.1 to 3.4 ms for medianIndex and from 33 to 22 ms for variance (29 to 37 fps); WebKit's is unchanged, and calls nothing reads cost Chrome about 1 ms more; the figure's markup and the answers are unchanged. A variance figure now takes `contain: layout`.

- `.dcss-format-si` rounds once, exactly, with no tables: 20 outputs moving every frame took 61 to 75 ms of rendering a frame in Safari, now 14 (Chrome 1.3 to 0.9), and `families/format.css` is 135 KB (237). Every digit is still d3's, including the 527 doubles that come closest to a tie past 10^22, now in the check.
- `.dcss-format-general` runs on the fixed format's exact rounding (d3's general notation is toPrecision, which rounds as toFixed does at its digits less one less the exponent): 20 outputs moving every frame took 70 ms of rendering a frame in Safari, now 11. It also prints a value that rounds up to 0.000001, which it left blank.
- The sum, mean, min, max and extent no longer round a value off the 1/64 grid to the nearest 64th: it leaves no answer, `--dcss-err` 1, as the median and variance already did (12.3 and 7.85 summed to 20.140625). An item with no number is skipped quietly by them too (`--dcss-err` 0, their `<output>` printing the answer), and a value past a call's range is error 1, not 2.
- Every call measures across and down whatever the page's `writing-mode` (in `vertical-rl` a median read -64,895.5).
- The median skips an item with no number, as d3 does, and so does the quantile at `--p` 0, 0.5 and 1; quantileSorted answers unless it reads one. Compiled, the median and the quantile at any `--p` skip it too (the compiler drops it from the literal data). Each item is laid out twice and a missing one sends a copy to each end, so the middle of the copies is the middle of the numbers. Their 1/64 steps now end at 131,071 (whole numbers still reach 8,257,535).
- Lists (`.list`, `.pager`), loops (`.loop-*`) and sheets (`.sheet` and its folds and formats) moved out of `dcss.css` into `dcss-extras.css`: their names have no `dcss-` prefix, so a page's own `.list` or `.sum` no longer gets their rules. A page that uses them links `dcss-extras.css`.
- The reductions take signed numbers. A sum lays its positives out across and its negatives down, so it takes any, with no offset. A mean takes values from -1024, each negative one offset by 1024; min, max and extent take values within ±131,072, or whole numbers to 8,257,535. An empty `<output>` prints d3.format's minus (U+2212). The sum's list no longer gives the count, mean and length, since its height holds the negatives: a mean's list gives the sum, count and length. A sum says nothing of an item it skips (d3 doesn't either).
- The sheet's own names (its grids, readers and internal numbers, `arrays-*` before) are `dcss-*`; the 40 that a call also used have names of their own (`.dcss-width-sum`, `--dcss-array-length`; the list is `src/names.mjs`'s `RENAMED`).

- The list median, quantile, quantileSorted and quantile scale take 1/64 steps within ±131,072 and whole numbers up to 8,257,535 (was ±1024).
- Stereographic points update about ten times faster, and mercator and equirectangular points a fifth faster.
- A median, quantile or quantileSorted of a list takes the list once, with three plain nested elements after it, in place of a second copy of the list inside `.dcss-order-upper`.
- A quantile scale takes its list once, with four plain nested elements after it, in place of three copies of the list and nine wrappers. It updates faster: a query change on 20 scales of 64 values took 115 ms in Chrome and 159 in Safari (272 and 265 before), one value changed 18 and 36 ms (154 and 206).
- Threshold and quantize scales take no list and no wrappers: a threshold scale's thresholds are `--domain-0` to `--domain-15`, a quantize scale's come from its domain, and everything inside the call reads the answer. A query change on 20 scales of 16 thresholds took 3.1 ms in Chrome and 4 in Safari for threshold (8.6 and 14 with a list), 4.4 and 7 for quantize (16.3 and 27). Thresholds out of order or with a gap fail (error 1).
- Cumsum's second list needs no class and its items no wrapper: everything inside the i-th item reads the i-th running total.
- A scale takes two stops unless it has the class `dcss-piecewise`: `--stops` and the stops past the second need it now. Two-stop scales update about four times faster.
- A list's length is counted in 8 `:has()` rules, one a binary digit, in place of one a length (65 for a median, 512 for the identity scale's ticks): 50 medians of 64 show in 51 ms in Chrome and 88 in Safari (62 and 134 before); `families/array.css` 431 to 394 KB, `ticks.css` 172 to 128 KB.
- Firefox: the compiled quantile and median (the layout way, `pipeline/quantile.mjs`) lay their 64ths out 64 px to the unit there, where the data's span is under 65,536, and read whole px back exact; before, any read with a fraction was error 1 there. The interpolation low + (high − low) × fr is worked out in parts there, within the float's step of the answer: the reads' whole 64ths, fr as two floats, the product exact by Dekker's method on Veltkamp's split for 24-bit floats, so the guard that refused any float interpolation not exact on the grid is gone (the calc way and quantileSorted too). Seeded in Firefox: quantile 141 right of 144 the layout way (16) and 135 the calc way (69), median 132 (18), quantileSorted 141 (28), the rest a printed tie the format guards; 0 wrong in all three engines, Chrome and WebKit unchanged.
- Firefox: `.dcss-format-fixed` and `.dcss-format-percent` print the float Firefox holds, within its step (DCSS.md rule 6), for values under 2^24 and up to 7 places: the integer part and the fraction's rounded digits are taken from the value's own text, in registered integers under 2^23. They refused (error 1) any value whose scaled |value| × 10^precision passed 2^22, a `.6f` of 4.2 or more, where d3's double might round otherwise. format-check judges Firefox by the float it holds for those types.
- `.dcss-bins.dcss-bin-max`: its rows are its bins (a child 1 px tall in row `--dcss-bin`, the grid 0 px tall), so a reader tells an empty bin (error 2) from one whose largest is 0 by its row, and the columns hold the maxima alone: the grid's width is their sum, exact. What is inside a child of a bin-max grid gets `--dcss-rank` 0 and `--dcss-err` 1; ranks are plain `.dcss-bins`' (A15).

### Removed

- `.dcss-ticks` and `.dcss-nice` (2026-11-02): a range's ticks and a range made nice are an axis's parts, and the axes left on 2026-10-31. `.dcss-bin` with `--count` still cuts where d3 does; `.dcss-range` stays.
- `mathml.mjs` (2026-10-31): Content MathML compiled to dcss calls is BASICSS's MathML format now, `libraries/basicss/mathml/` in the noscript.ing repository, with its checks and SPEC requirements (A131 to A179 here, M131 to M179 there). The page build it ran first is `steps.mjs`.
- Charts, maps and easing (2026-10-31: dcss is for pages that compute): the axes (`.dcss-axis-*`), `families/shape.css` (pie, arc, stack, line, area), `families/hierarchy.css` (treemaps, partition), `families/geo.css` and the raw projections, `families/ease.css`; the band, point, radial, symlog, sequential and diverging scales; a log scale's ticks, nice and labels (`.dcss-ticks-log`, `.dcss-nice-log`, `.dcss-ticks-log-format`); the identity scale's ticks (`.dcss-identity-ticks`). CSS's own `linear()` and `cubic-bezier()` ease. git keeps every port.
- The typed calls (`families/aggregate.css`, `order.css` and `extrema.css`, and `.dcss-variance` and `.dcss-deviation` in `array.css`): values given as `--type-N` and `--value-N` on one element have no boxes to lay out, so each call was an arithmetic machine emulating d3's doubles, nulls, strings and objects, 5.6 MB in all. Lists on the grid are the one data model: sum, count, mean, cumsum, min, max, extent, the index extrema, median, quantile and quantileSorted take a list. Gone with them: fsum, fcumsum, mode, scan, variance and deviation, and values that aren't numbers on the grid. `families/` is 1.3 MB in all (86 KB gzipped), `array.css` 393 to 149 KB.
- Colors (`families/color.css`, `color-text.css`): gamutant.css reads a CSS color's channels as numbers, and CSS's `color-mix()` and relative colors convert and interpolate them.
- Date parsing (`families/date-parse.css`, `date-parse-local.css`): its text came as character codes, and the program that writes the codes holds the date as a number.
- Pacific time (`.dcss-local-*` intervals and `.dcss-local-date-format-N`): one fixed zone, America/Los_Angeles, that d3's tests use. Time is UTC: `time.css` 1.6 MB to 315 KB, `date-format.css` 85 to 36 KB.
- `.dcss-pack-five` (d3.packSiblings of five circles): `hierarchy.css` 2.55 MB to 6.5 KB.
- `.dcss-geo-mercator-fit-extent` and `.dcss-geo-transverse-mercator-fit-extent`: `geo.css` 1.6 MB to 248 KB.
- `.dcss-random-lcg` (`families/random.css`).
- `.dcss-call-input`: a call takes another's answer written straight inside it. A call with no answer publishes no number, so the next fails too and prints nothing.
- The markup that wrote a list two or three times, and the named wrappers: `.dcss-call-read`, `.dcss-call-result`, `.dcss-range-copy`, `.dcss-order-upper`, `.dcss-source-order`, `.dcss-quantile-low`, `.dcss-quantile-high` and `.dcss-scan-results`, and a threshold or quantize scale's list. Every call takes its data once, read through plain elements or none; a page with those classes gets no answer, and the compiler stops with an error on them. `families/array.css` is 24 KB smaller (431 KB), and its parts shrink with it: the median part is 2.4 KB gzipped (4.2 before).

### Fixed
- Firefox: a sum's or max's value written with a dot (a price) answers in the list's decimals, two by default, as a mean's does, each value to 41,943.03 (its k under 2^22, where Firefox's float still tells a value off the grid); it was error 1 for any fraction. Its reductions' bounds are stated (QUIRKS.md Q25): a mean's magnitudes total 2,031,615 at most, a min's, extent's or decimals max's k 950,276, a whole max 1,900,543.

- A format given NaN or infinity printed 0 with error 0: an infinite value's split parts went NaN in the bound flag, and for a day the en-IN term's NaN voided the flag for NaN too. A format with no number prints nothing with error 1, in WebKit after a number too, where the `::after`'s registered flag stayed at its last value.
- A chain past a call's bounds (a median of 2.001 feeding a format) printed a number with error 0: the same fault.
- In WebKit, an infinite cumsum value made its item's width NaN with no error (0 × infinity in a registered integer that keeps infinity); in Firefox, many failed cumsum values wrapped the tracks' sum past 2^31 app units and read a total with error 0; in Chrome, two failed share values collapsed the grid past 2^25 px to error 2. Each is caught: a failed value is clamped, laid out in one track, or read as under 1 px.
- In Firefox, `.dcss-lookup` and a reduction with `--find` took a row whose key was the neighbour of `--find` past 2^24 (16777217 found 16777216's value, error 0), since Firefox reads such numbers as 32-bit floats. The lookup now matches 8-digit and 13-digit keys exactly and gives error 1 for the few it can't tell apart; a reduction fails (error 1) on a row whose key is that close.
- A `.dcss-solve` nested in another's answer never gave error 1: with no formula or no bracket of its own it gave `--dcss-ready` 1, its flags carried in from the outer solve (a registered flag that can't be computed inherits). Its bracket and flags now start empty on it; its answer is cleared on its first `<i>`, so it can read the outer answer on itself; its `<i>`s draw no boxes and leave the font alone (they were italic, 2,484 boxes on the check's page).
- arccot of a name past 2^996 (up to 2^1000) had its answer from 1/x rounded once: splitting x to multiply it exactly passed the largest double, and 1/x lost its correction. arccot there now has no answer, and arctan takes 2^996's, π/2.
- A sum, mean, min, max or extent of whole numbers from 1,000,000 up was rounded to 6 digits in Chrome with no error (sum(1234567) gave 1,234,570, max(1234567, 3) 1,234,570): Chrome passes a registered integer through `var()` with 6 significant digits, so each value now travels as three integers of 6 digits at most. A sum or mean whose list reaches 2^24 px gives no answer (`--dcss-err` 1): sum(16777216, 16777216) gave 0 in Chrome, whose grid is 0 px wide past 2^25, and 33,554,431.984375 in Safari. A min, max or extent past 8,257,535, the documented range, gives error 1, not error 2 (Chrome's padding lost its 1 px there).
- A share whose `<li>`s hold text directly (`<li style="--value: 3">Apples <div class="dcss-share-read">…`) or take a page's padding or `::before` margin gave every share of a wrong total (3, 1, 4 totalled 62.66), with no error; the text and the page's rules now take no room in the sum. probes/dcss/pages-check.mjs runs the 15 list calls README has no example of labelled and under a site's list rules.
- A page's own rules on lists moved the static calls' answers, with no error, in Chrome and Safari: `li::before { margin-right: .25rem }` a median's, quantile's or variance's, `li { padding: 0 .25rem }` a cumsum's or bin's. The boxes those calls measure now take no margin, padding, border, float or position from the page; readme-check runs every example again under a site's usual list rules.
- A median, quantile, quantile scale, median or quantile index or variance whose `<li>`s hold text (a label) answered wrong with no error, in Chrome and Safari: the median of 1, 2, 3, 4 gave -65,498.7, the variance of 1 to 5 -654,740. Each item's text made an anonymous box in the list's grid, a line tall and in the items' sorted order; the hidden lists now give text no size and sort it after every item, and an item's elements no box. README's examples run again with labelled items in readme-check.
- A sum, mean, min, max or extent whose list passes 262,144 px with fractions gives no answer (`--dcss-err` 1): Chrome read sum(262144, 0.015625) as 262,144 with no error, and Safari a min 1/64 low. Whole numbers written in the items stay exact while the list is under 2^24 px.
- `--dcss-mean` is d3's mean to the bit: it was rounded to millionths (mean(1, 2, 4) gave 2.333333, d3 2.3333333333333335).

- A scale's answer given to another scale as a domain stop or exponent gave no value in Safari (it passed Safari's 65,535 tokens a `var()`); a scale's answer is now about 400 tokens. Moving scales cost up to a fifth more in Safari for it, nothing more in Chrome.
